Service · 01

Tracing a Leak

Water on a flat roof does not fall, it travels. It runs along the deck to a low spot, sits there, and goes through at a seam or a fastener that can be forty feet from the ceiling tile it eventually drips onto. Chasing the stain finds nothing.

  • Ponding mapped before anything is opened
  • Seams, laps and fasteners checked in order
  • Findings photographed for your file

Low-slope systems

What Is Actually on Your Roof

Flat is not a material, it is a category, and the five systems inside it are not interchangeable. Ponding water finishes one of them and barely troubles another. Grease from a kitchen extract will destroy a membrane that is fine on a warehouse. We spec for the building, not for the category.

System 01
01

Built-Up Roofing

The layered felt-and-bitumen system still sitting on a lot of older New Orleans buildings, usually under gravel. Heavy, repairable, and frequently worth repairing rather than replacing. The failures are almost always at the flashings and the drains, not in the field.

Repairable well past what owners expectFails at the edges, not the middle
More on this →
System 02
02

Modified Bitumen and Torch-Down

Rolled sheets with a polymer-modified asphalt, heat-welded at the laps. Tough underfoot, which matters on a roof that carries HVAC units and gets walked on. The seams are the whole job, and a bad lap will find you in the first heavy rain.

Stands up to foot traffic and equipmentThe lap is the job
More on this →
System 03
03

Single-Ply: TPO, PVC and EPDM

The default on most new low-slope work. Light, fast to install and reflective in the white membranes, which matters in Gulf heat. Each of the three behaves differently around grease, ponding and chemical exposure, which is why a restaurant and a warehouse should not get the same spec.

Reflective, which shows on the cooling billSpecified per building, not per catalog
More on this →

04

Elastomeric Coating

Not a roof on its own. A seamless liquid membrane over a sound one, adding years of life and reflecting heat, for a fraction of what a replacement costs. It only works if what is underneath is dry and sound, so we survey before we ever recommend it.

A fraction of replacement costOnly over a roof that is genuinely sound

01 Scope

Replacement Is Not Always
the Right Answer

A sound flat roof with a failing surface does not need tearing off. A coating over it adds years and reflects heat off the building, and it lands nowhere near a replacement on cost or on disruption.

Full replacement Tear-off, disposal, new system, most disruption
Coating a sound roof Years added, heat reflected, a fraction of the cost

The survey decides which one your roof is. If the insulation under the membrane is wet, a coating traps the problem and we will tell you that rather than sell you one. If it is dry, coating is very often the honest recommendation.

02 Record

Your Maintenance History
Is Part of Your Claim

Insurers increasingly ask what was done to the roof and when. A building with two documented visits a year and photographs of each one is in a very different position after a storm than a building with nothing.

Roof with no maintenance record Harder claim
Roof with two documented visits a year Documented position

We are roofers, not public adjusters, and we will not promise you an outcome. What we can do is make sure there is a record, and that the record is in a format somebody else can actually read.
