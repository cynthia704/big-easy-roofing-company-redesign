Two Things About Louisiana That Catch Homeowners Out

Neither of these is about roofing. Both change what a storm-damaged roof ends up costing you.

The Clock Starts at the Storm, Not at the Stain

Louisiana puts a time limit on claims for storm damage, and it runs from the date of the loss, not from the day you noticed the ceiling. The limit changed in 2024, so what applied to a storm three seasons ago is not what applies to one this year. If a named storm has been over your roof and nobody has been up there since, do not assume the window has closed, and do not assume it is still open. Check your policy and the current deadline before you decide either way.

[EDITOR: do not publish a specific number of years here. Louisiana Civil Code art. 3493.12 moved damage to immovable property to a two-year prescriptive period for losses on or after 1 July 2024; the old one-year rule still applies before that date, and policy terms can differ again. Have a lawyer confirm the exact wording before this goes live.]

Older Roofs Are Often Paid at Actual Cash Value

That means depreciation comes off the payout. A twenty-year-old roof can be fully covered and still leave a gap between what the insurer pays and what the job costs. Knowing which one your policy carries changes how you plan.

We are not insurance adjusters and we will not tell you your claim is guaranteed. What we do is document the damage thoroughly enough that your adjuster has everything they need to price it properly.
