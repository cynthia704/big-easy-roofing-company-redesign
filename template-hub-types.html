Roofing materials · New Orleans

The right roofing material in New Orleans is not the right material in Denver or Phoenix. Heat, humidity, heavy rain and hurricane wind narrow the field considerably.

Some materials that are perfectly sensible elsewhere are a slow mistake here, and we would rather say so at the estimate than at the first named storm. This page is the plain version: what holds up on a Gulf Coast property, what each option asks of you, and where the honest answer is to repair what you already have.

I Want the Longest Life

Metal and slate are the two that outlast everything else here. Standing seam metal costs more upfront and gives you a roof that will outlive two or three shingle roofs, with very few places for wind to get a grip. Slate lasts longer still and is already on a lot of historic New Orleans homes.

  • Standing seam metal, or slate where it already exists
  • Higher upfront, far fewer roofs over a lifetime
  • Both need a deck that can carry them

What actually decides it

Four Things That Narrow the Field

Materials get chosen on looks and budget. They get judged, a few years later, on these four. Working through them first is how you end up with a roof you do not think about.

Factor 01
01

Wind, and How It Actually Gets In

Wind does not crush a roof, it peels one. It finds an edge with a gap under it and lifts. That is why the starter strip at the eave, the nailing pattern, and the ridge cap matter more than the shingle you picked. A premium shingle nailed high is a worse roof than a mid-range shingle nailed correctly.

Edges and fasteners, not the fieldInstall decides the ratingDecides the warranty

Factor 02
02

Heat and Humidity

Gulf summers cook a roof from above and a badly ventilated attic cooks it from below. That combination ages asphalt faster here than the same product ages in a dry climate, and it is very hard on cedar. Reflective surfaces and real attic airflow buy back years on any material you choose.

Ventilation is part of the material choiceHard on cedar, kind to metal

Weight, and What Your Deck Can Carry

Tile and slate are heavy enough that the structure has to be checked before the material is chosen, not after. This is the first thing we look at rather than the last, because finding out late is how a project stops halfway through with the roof open.

On a historic property the existing material is often the right answer. Original slate frequently has decades left, and matching a repair costs a fraction of replacing a roof that has not failed. Why There Are No Lifespan Charts on This Page

You will find plenty of sites that will tell you a shingle roof lasts exactly so many years. The honest answer is that the same product on two New Orleans houses, one ventilated properly and one not, will not last the same length of time, and a number on a chart hides that.

What we will do is walk your roof, tell you what condition the one you have is in, and give you a written estimate for the options that genuinely suit it.

Two things worth knowing first

The Material Matters Less
Than People Think

Two decisions change the outcome more than the name on the bundle does.

01 Install

The Cheapest Install Is
the Most Expensive Roof

Wind rating is not a property of a shingle sitting in a warehouse. It is a property of that shingle nailed in the right place, over a starter strip, with the right accessories around it. Nail the same premium shingle an inch too high and the rating is gone.

Correctly nailed system Rating intact, warranty intact
Same shingle, nailed high Rating gone, and so is the manufacturer cover

This is why two quotes for "the same shingle" can be thousands apart and both be honest. Ask what the nailing pattern is, ask whether starter strip is included, and ask who is actually on the roof.
